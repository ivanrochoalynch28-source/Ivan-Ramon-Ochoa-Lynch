A little about me

Hi, my name is Ivan Ramón Ochoa Lynch. I’m a 20-year-old student-athlete from Bloomington, Indiana, pursuing a double major in Economics and Hispanic Studies at Dartmouth College, with a focus on development economics. I’m also a Division I athlete, competing in discus and hammer for the men’s track and field team.

I’ve always been motivated by things that are intellectually stimulating, give me something to chase, and make me feel passionate. If I’ve ended up crossing your path in some way, chances are it was in pursuit of one of those things.

I strongly believe that every person on this planet is capable of things they couldn’t even imagine if given the right time, opportunities, and resources. I care deeply about personal freedom and economic mobility, and I hope to build a career bringing capital, people, and ideas together where they can accomplish something meaningful. I’m particularly interested in investment, advising, economic development, and the ways financial resources can help worthwhile businesses and organizations grow into something extraordinary.

Welcome to my website. Here you’ll find a little bit of everything: my academic and professional experiences, athletics, personal passions and adventures, writing, creative projects, and photographs that offer a glimpse into who I am beyond my résumé.

I’m excited about where the world will take me, the wonderful and capable people I’ll meet, and the amazing things we can all accomplish together along the way.

Socios En Salud

Global Health and Economic Development Intern

Sep. 2026 – Present
Lima, Peru

I’m supporting community health programs through Dartmouth’s Dickey Center for International Understanding internship program. I began with an anemia prevention program in Pachacútec and now work with TB VIDA to understand how substance use, alcoholism, depression, and anxiety affect treatment adherence among patients with tuberculosis in Carabayllo and Comas in northern Lima.

The work spans different parts of Peru’s health system, from direct, in-person implementation to organizing and analyzing program data to support evaluation, donor reporting, and funding. I’ve been able to work in Spanish with health professionals, community leaders, and patients while seeing how the decisions made about a program translate into people’s everyday lives.

Gamma Delta Chi

House Manager

June – Aug. 2026
Hanover, NH

Over the summer, I was responsible for helping keep a house of more than 40 residents running. That meant managing a budget of more than $12,000, coordinating maintenance, meeting building compliance requirements, and working with contractors, college staff, and the people living in the house.

It was a practical kind of leadership: noticing what needed to be fixed, figuring out how to pay for it, and following through. I wanted the house to be a better place to live, and I had to learn how to turn that intention into decisions about time, money, and other people’s needs.

Dartmouth College

Economics Teaching Assistant

Jan. – Apr. 2026
Hanover, NH

I was selected as one of six teaching assistants from more than 100 applicants for Dartmouth’s introductory economics course. In office hours with around 30 students, I helped explain microeconomic theory, price mechanisms, and quantitative problems through mathematical and graphical analysis.

I enjoyed the process of finding a different way to explain something until it made sense to someone else. Teaching gave me another reason to understand the material well, and it was rewarding to watch students become more confident working through ideas that had initially felt difficult.

Indiana Track Club

Head Throws Coach

June – Aug. 2025
Bloomington, IN

I spent the summer coaching 22 middle and high school athletes in the throws, creating individual training plans and leading daily practices. The athletes came with different levels of experience, different ways of learning, and different things they wanted from the sport.

I loved taking something I had spent years trying to understand and helping someone else make it their own. Coaching meant learning to recognize what each athlete needed, adjusting when something wasn’t working, and sharing the excitement when their progress began to show.
